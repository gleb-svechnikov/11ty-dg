Marble Open-Sources a Skill Taxonomy of 1,590 Elementary Learning Topics Wired by Prerequisites

What it is

Marble Skill Taxonomy is an open, structured dataset describing what children actually learn across the primary and elementary years. Version 1 breaks the curriculum into 1,590 "micro-topics" — single teachable ideas like "Building sentences" or "Apparent brightness of stars" — and wires them into a directed acyclic graph of 3,221 prerequisite edges, each tagged hard or soft with a one-line reason. Eight subjects are covered, led by science (547 topics) and mathematics (503). Every topic carries a plain-language description, mastery evidence criteria, a type, an approximate age range, and links back to the curriculum standards it was distilled from, including NGSS, Common Core and the UK National Curriculum.

Highlights

  • A graph, not a list. Most curriculum data is either a flat list of standards or locked inside a product. This dataset models learning relationships explicitly, and adds 183 parent-friendly one-paragraph summaries per subject, domain and age band.
  • Careful licensing. The database is ODbL 1.0 — free for research and commercial use, attribution required, with share-alike applying to derivative databases, not to products built on top of it. Authored text is CC BY-SA 4.0, while extracted curriculum standards keep their upstream licenses, documented in PROVENANCE.md.
  • Pure data. No runtime, no dependencies — just UTF-8 JSON, with JSON Schemas, a manifest carrying SHA-256 checksums, and a validation script. The project's main language is JavaScript, and 5,839 stars with 981 forks suggest plenty of interest.

Getting started

Clone the repository and read the files in data/: topics.json for nodes, dependencies.json for edges, curriculum-standards.json for alignment and clusters.json for summaries. The official docs include a short JavaScript snippet that imports the JSON directly and lists a topic's prerequisites by ID; run node scripts/validate.mjs to check structure and referential integrity. If you'd rather look before you code, an interactive 3D visualization lives at withmarble.com/curriculum. Any use requires the specified attribution line.

Who it's for

Ed-tech developers building adaptive or tutoring products, education researchers, curriculum designers, and anyone visualizing or analyzing learning progressions — provided they can live with attribution and share-alike terms.
